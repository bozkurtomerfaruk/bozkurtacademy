What does level A1 mean?

A1 is the first level of the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR). Someone who has completed A1 can introduce themselves, ask and answer simple questions, understand very common everyday expressions and fill in short forms. For example:

  • Ich heiße Ayşe. Ich komme aus der Türkei. (My name is Ayşe. I’m from Türkiye.)
  • Wo wohnen Sie? (Where do you live?)
  • Ich möchte einen Termin machen. (I would like to make an appointment.)

A1 is the starting point for anyone who doesn’t speak any German yet. If you have learned a little German before but your foundations have gaps, we can quickly review the A1 topics and fill those gaps. If you are wondering where to start, have a look at the post How to learn German (in Turkish).

A1 German for family reunification and visas

If you need a German certificate for family reunification or a visa application, you can prepare in the lessons for beginner-level exams (for example the exams of the Goethe-Institut, telc or ÖSD).

Which level and which certificate are required, any exceptions and the current rules can vary depending on the type of application. So before you apply, always check the latest information on the official website of the German embassy or consulate responsible for you, or with the authority you are applying to. In the post German for family reunification: where to start (in Turkish), I explain how to begin preparing for A1.

A1 exams usually have four parts:

  • Listening (Hören): announcements, short phone messages and everyday dialogues
  • Reading (Lesen): short adverts, signs and emails
  • Writing (Schreiben): filling in a form and writing a short message
  • Speaking (Sprechen): introducing yourself, asking and answering questions, making a request

When preparing for the exam, we work through the task types of each part one by one. For the speaking part in particular, we practise phrases for introducing yourself and the task of asking questions using cards many times. The site also has free practice sets for the Goethe A1 reading part. For details about the exam, see the German exam preparation page. You will find study tips for each part in the post How to pass the A1 German exam (in Turkish).

Topics you will learn in the A1 course

We go from simple to more difficult topics. Each topic has a free explanation and exercises on this site (in Turkish):

The basics

  • The German alphabet and pronunciation (game)
  • Articles: der, die, das
  • Plural nouns
  • sein and haben
  • Numbers, times and dates

Building sentences

  • Präsens: the present tense
  • W-Fragen: questions
  • Negation with nicht and kein
  • Separable verbs

Everyday communication

  • Modal verbs: können, müssen, wollen
  • Akkusativ: the direct object
  • mein, dein, sein, ihr
  • Perfekt: talking about the past

How do we start the A1 lessons?

  1. Free first lesson

    We talk about whether you have come across German before, about your goal and, if you have an exam, when it is.

  2. Plan and pace

    Together we decide on the number of lessons per week and which topics we need to cover before the exam.

  3. Topics and practice

    In every lesson you learn one topic and then practise speaking and writing with situations from everyday life.

  4. Exam rehearsal

    Before the exam we practise the task types under timed conditions and rehearse the speaking part.

How long does it take to learn A1 German?

That depends on how many lessons you take per week, how much you review between lessons and whether you have learned another foreign language before. With regular lessons and short daily reviews, progress is much faster. If you have an exam date, we draw up a realistic plan for it in the first lesson.

The fee depends on whether you choose one-to-one or group lessons and on the number of lessons per week. For details, write to me.

Questions about the A1 German course

I don’t speak any German. Can I start the A1 course?

Yes, A1 is designed for complete beginners. We move through the topics step by step with simple examples.

Which exam should I take for family reunification?

Which certificate is accepted can vary depending on the type of application. Before you apply, check the current requirement on the official website of the responsible embassy or consulate; in the lessons we then prepare for the task types of that exam.

Which part of the A1 exam do learners find hardest?

Learners usually find the listening and speaking parts the hardest. That is why we set aside extra time in the lessons for listening tasks and for speaking tasks such as introducing yourself and asking questions.

What should I do after A1?

Levels A2 and B1 are important for managing everyday life in Germany with confidence. Once you have finished A1, you can continue with the A2 German course.
