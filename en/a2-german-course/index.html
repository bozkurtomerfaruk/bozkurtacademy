What can you do at level A2?

According to the CEFR, A2 is the second stage of the “basic user” level. Someone who has completed A2 can have short conversations on familiar topics such as shopping, work, family and their surroundings, describe past experiences in simple terms and write short personal messages. For example:

  • Am Wochenende habe ich meine Freunde besucht. (I visited my friends at the weekend.)
  • Ich lerne Deutsch, weil ich in Deutschland arbeiten möchte. (I’m learning German because I want to work in Germany.)
  • Könnten Sie mir bitte helfen? (Could you help me, please?)

At A1 you build short, simple sentences. At A2 you learn to link sentences with conjunctions such as “weil”, “dass” and “wenn”, which immediately makes your speech sound much more natural.

A2 for everyday life in Germany

For someone who has just moved to Germany, A2 is the level at which you start handling everyday tasks on your own. Making a doctor’s appointment, exchanging short messages with your landlord, understanding a simple note from your child’s school or having a short chat with colleagues at work all become possible with A2.

In the lessons we act out these situations: making an appointment by phone, replying to an email, filling in a form. Learning grammar through these real tasks makes the rules easier to remember.

If you are preparing for an A2 exam (for example the Goethe-Zertifikat A2 or telc Deutsch A2), we work through the task types one by one. The site also has free practice sets for the Goethe and telc A2 reading parts. You will find the details on the exam preparation page.

How are the lessons planned?

In the free first lesson we check whether there are any gaps in your A1 topics. If there are, we close them first, because A2 builds on A1. Then we set an order based on your goal and decide together whether to focus on speaking, an exam or writing.

You can take the lessons as private one-to-one lessons or as group classes with learners at the same level. The fee depends on the format and the number of lessons per week. For details, write to me.

How should you study between lessons at A2?

At A2, the fastest progress comes from short reviews done every day. A few suggestions:

  • Learn words in sentences: instead of just “der Termin”, write a full sentence such as “Ich habe morgen einen Termin.” (I have an appointment tomorrow.) That way you remember the article and the usage together.
  • Describe your day in German: in the evening, write three sentences in the Perfekt: what you did, where you went and who you talked to.
  • Do some short listening: listen to a few minutes of slow news or a podcast and note down the words you understand.
  • Do exercises: read the explanation for each answer in the A2 exercises on this site (in Turkish). Do the questions you got wrong again the next day.

Questions about the A2 German course

Can I start A2 before finishing A1?

A2 builds on A1, so we check your level first. If a few A1 topics are missing, we can quickly cover them at the beginning of the A2 lessons.

Can I work in Germany with A2?

That depends on the type of job and the employer. A2 gives you a good foundation for everyday communication. The required level and the accepted certificates can vary depending on the type of application, the institution and the time. Always check the current requirement with an official source.

What is the hardest topic at A2?

Learners usually find it hardest to tell Dativ and Akkusativ apart and to get adjective endings right. We work on these topics with plenty of examples and short, frequent reviews.
