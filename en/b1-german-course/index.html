Why is level B1 so important?

B1 is the first stage of the “independent user” level in the CEFR. Someone who reaches B1 understands the main points of conversations on familiar topics such as work, school and leisure, can handle most situations that arise while travelling, describe experiences and plans, and briefly give reasons for their opinions.

With B1 you start using German more independently in everyday life, at work and in education. Which level and which certificate are required for vocational training, a job application or official procedures depends on the institution and the process. So check the current information for your situation with the institution you are applying to.

A few examples of B1 sentences:

  • Obwohl ich müde war, bin ich zum Kurs gegangen. (Although I was tired, I went to the class.)
  • Das ist die Firma, bei der ich mich beworben habe. (This is the company I applied to.)
  • Ich finde es wichtig, jeden Tag ein bisschen zu üben. (I think it’s important to practise a little every day.)

We work on all four skills together

Speaking: at B1, short answers are no longer enough. We practise giving your opinion on a topic in several sentences, making plans together and giving reasons. This helps you both in the speaking part of the exam and in job interviews.

Writing: a formal email, a letter of complaint or a comment in an online forum are typical B1 tasks. In class we correct your texts together and identify the mistakes you make most often.

Reading and listening: with short texts taken from news articles, adverts and radio programmes, we practise finding the main idea and the details. The site has free reading practice sets for Goethe B1, telc B1 and the DTZ.

If you are preparing for the Goethe-Zertifikat B1, telc Deutsch B1 or the DTZ (Deutsch-Test für Zuwanderer, the German test for immigrants), we organise the lessons around the exam’s task types. You will find the details on the exam preparation page. For the differences between Goethe B1 and telc B1, see the post Goethe or telc? The differences between the German exams (in Turkish).

How are the lessons planned?

Before starting B1, we look for gaps in the A2 topics. Dativ, adjective endings and verb position in subordinate clauses in particular come up again and again at B1. Then we make a plan based on your goal: around your exam date if you have an exam, or around the conversations and correspondence of your field if you are aiming for a job or vocational training.

You can take the lessons as one-to-one or group lessons. The fee depends on the format and the number of lessons per week. For details, write to me.

How should you study between lessons at B1?

  • Write one text every week: write an email or a short opinion piece. We correct it together in class, and you collect your most frequent mistakes in a list.
  • Use connectors deliberately: build a few sentences every day with connectors such as “obwohl”, “trotzdem” and “deshalb”. This makes your speech more fluent and connected.
  • Consume German content: watch German videos or read news on a topic you enjoy. First try to understand the main idea, then the details.
  • Speak out loud: choose a topic, speak German about it for one minute and record yourself. When you listen back, note where you got stuck.

Questions about the B1 German course

Is B1 enough to work in Germany?

That depends on the profession, the employer and the process. Always check the required level with the employer or the relevant institution; in the lessons we make a plan based on your goal.

Which part of the B1 exam do learners find hardest?

Learners usually find the writing and speaking parts the hardest, because there you are expected to explain your opinion and give reasons. In the lessons we practise these tasks with ready-to-use phrases and plenty of practice.

What is the difference between the DTZ and Goethe B1?

The DTZ is an exam designed for immigrants in Germany, while the Goethe-Zertifikat B1 is a general language exam. Which one you take depends on what you need the certificate for; check the current information with the exam provider and with the institution you are applying to.
