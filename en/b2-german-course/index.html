What can you do at level B2?

B2 is the second stage of the “independent user” level in the CEFR. Someone who reaches B2 understands the main ideas of complex texts on abstract topics, can follow technical discussions in their own field and can talk with native German speakers without strain for either side. They can write clear, detailed texts on a topic and discuss the advantages and disadvantages of different options.

When moving from B1 to B2, the difference is usually this: you already know what you want to say; now you learn how to say it more accurately and more appropriately. For example:

  • Je mehr ich spreche, desto sicherer werde ich. (The more I speak, the more confident I become.)
  • Der Antrag muss bis Freitag eingereicht werden. (The application must be submitted by Friday.)
  • Der Sprecher sagte, das Projekt sei erfolgreich gewesen. (The spokesperson said the project had been successful.)

Who needs B2?

B2 is an important level for anyone who wants to use German more fluently and accurately at work, in their profession and at university. Which level and which certificate are required for a job, professional recognition, vocational training or a university application depends on the institution, the federal state and the profession. Always check the current requirements of the relevant institution before you apply.

If you work in engineering or another technical field, we can also work on technical German in the lessons: the language of meetings, reports and email correspondence, and the vocabulary of your field. Because I am continuing my engineering studies in Germany and have worked in production, quality and technical projects, I can use real examples here.

Topics you will work on in the B2 course

Advanced grammar

  • Konjunktiv I: reported speech
  • Passive with modal verbs
  • Partizip I and Partizip II
  • je … desto

Formal and academic language

  • Nominalisierung: turning verbs into nouns
  • Prepositions with the genitive
  • Formal emails and application letters
  • Describing charts and tables

Speaking and discussion

  • Giving your opinion and responding to counter-arguments
  • Giving presentations and answering questions
  • The language of job interviews and meetings
  • Taking notes from long listening texts

Preparing for B2 exams

The Goethe-Zertifikat B2 and telc Deutsch B2 are the exams most often used to certify level B2. Both have reading, listening, writing and speaking parts. telc B2 also has a “Sprachbausteine” part that tests grammar and vocabulary.

When preparing for the exam, we practise the task types of each part under timed conditions and correct your writing tasks together according to the assessment criteria. The site has free practice sets for the Goethe B2 reading part and for the telc B2 reading and Sprachbausteine parts. You will find the details on the exam preparation page.

You can take the lessons as one-to-one or group lessons. For B2 exam preparation, one-to-one lessons are usually more effective, because personal feedback is essential for the writing and speaking tasks. For fees, write to me.

Questions about the B2 German course

Why does moving from B1 to B2 feel difficult?

At B2 you learn not only new rules but also how to express the same idea in different, more formal ways. Your vocabulary also needs to grow quickly. That is why regular reading, listening and writing practice is especially important at B2.

Can I study at a university in Germany with B2?

Language requirements vary by university and degree programme. Check the language requirement on the website of the university you are applying to; in the lessons we make a plan based on your goal.

Can I start technical German before B2?

Yes. The vocabulary and correspondence of your field can be added to the lessons from level B1. At B2 we move on to broader topics such as the language of meetings, reports and presentations.
