What does A1 mean?

A1 is the first level of the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR). At the end of A1 you can introduce yourself, ask and answer simple questions, understand very common everyday expressions and fill in short forms. For example:

  • Ich heiße Anna. Ich komme aus England. (My name is Anna. I’m from England.)
  • Wo wohnen Sie? (Where do you live?)
  • Ich möchte einen Termin machen. (I’d like to make an appointment.)

A1 is the starting point if you have never learned German. If you have learned a little before but your basics are shaky, we review the A1 topics quickly and fill the gaps.

A1 for family reunification and visas

If you need a German certificate for a family reunification or other visa application, you can prepare for the beginner exams in class – for example from the Goethe-Institut, telc or ÖSD.

Which level and certificate are required, which exceptions apply and what the current rules are depends on your application. Always check the official website of the German mission in your country, or the authority handling your application, before you apply.

A1 exams usually have four parts:

  • Listening (Hören): announcements, short phone messages and everyday conversations
  • Reading (Lesen): short ads, signs and emails
  • Writing (Schreiben): filling in a form and writing a short message
  • Speaking (Sprechen): introducing yourself, asking and answering questions, making a request

We practise each task type separately, especially introducing yourself and the question cards in the speaking part. There are free practice sets for the Goethe A1 reading section on the website. More on the exam preparation page.

What you learn in the A1 course

We go from simple to harder topics:

Basics

  • The alphabet and pronunciation
  • Articles: der, die, das
  • Plural nouns
  • sein and haben
  • Numbers, times and dates

Building sentences

  • Present tense
  • W-questions
  • Negation with nicht and kein
  • Separable verbs

Everyday communication

  • Modal verbs: können, müssen, wollen
  • The accusative
  • mein, dein, sein, ihr
  • Perfekt: talking about the past

How we start

  1. Free first lesson

    We talk about any German you already know, your goal and, if you have one, your exam date.

  2. Plan and pace

    We agree how many lessons a week make sense and which topics to cover before the exam.

  3. Topic and practice

    One topic per lesson, followed by speaking and writing in everyday situations.

  4. Dress rehearsal

    Before the exam we practise the task types against the clock and rehearse the speaking part.

How long does A1 take?

It depends on how many lessons you take per week, how much you review in between and whether you have learned another foreign language before. Regular lessons and short daily reviews make a big difference. If your exam date is fixed, we make a realistic plan in the first lesson.

The price depends on whether you choose one-to-one or group lessons and on the number of lessons per week. Just send me a message via the contact form.

Questions about the A1 course

I don’t speak any German. Can I start the A1 course?

Yes, A1 is exactly for complete beginners. We go step by step with simple examples, and I explain in English.

Which exam do I need for family reunification?

Which certificate is accepted can depend on your application. Check the current requirement on the official website of the relevant German mission before you apply; in class we prepare for the task types of that exam.

Which part of the A1 exam is hardest?

Most learners find listening and speaking hardest, so we spend extra time on listening tasks and on introducing yourself and asking questions.

What comes after A1?

For everyday life in Germany, A2 and B1 matter. After A1 you can continue with the German course A2.
