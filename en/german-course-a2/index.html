You’ve finished A1 – now it’s time for longer sentences and talking about the past. The A2 course builds the base you need to handle most everyday situations in German.

What can you do at A2?

A2 is the second step of the CEFR’s “basic user” level. At A2 you can have short conversations about familiar topics such as shopping, work, family and your surroundings, describe past events in simple terms and write short personal messages. For example:

  • Am Wochenende habe ich meine Freunde besucht. (I visited my friends at the weekend.)
  • Ich lerne Deutsch, weil ich in Deutschland arbeiten möchte. (I’m learning German because I want to work in Germany.)
  • Könnten Sie mir bitte helfen? (Could you help me, please?)

At A1 you make short, separate sentences. At A2 you learn to join them with words like “weil”, “dass” and “wenn” – and suddenly you sound much more natural.

What you learn in the A2 course

Nouns and cases

  • The dative
  • Verbs with two objects
  • Two-way prepositions
  • Adjective endings

Joining sentences

  • Subordinate clauses: weil, dass, wenn
  • Comparatives and superlatives
  • Verbs with prepositions
  • Reflexive verbs

Tenses and politeness

  • Präteritum
  • Future tense
  • Konjunktiv II: polite requests
  • Perfekt review

A2 for everyday life in Germany

If you’ve just moved to Germany, A2 is the level where you start handling everyday tasks on your own: booking a doctor’s appointment, writing a short message to your landlord, understanding a simple note from your child’s school or chatting with colleagues.

We act out exactly these situations in class: making an appointment by phone, replying to an email, filling in a form. Grammar learned inside real tasks is much easier to remember.

If you are preparing for an A2 exam (such as Goethe-Zertifikat A2 or telc Deutsch A2), we practise each task type. There are free practice sets for the Goethe and telc A2 reading sections. More on the exam preparation page.

How the lessons are planned

In the free first lesson we check for gaps in the A1 topics. If there are any, we close them first, because A2 builds on A1. Then we set an order based on your goal – more speaking, an exam or more writing.

You can take the lessons one-to-one or in a group with learners at the same level. The price depends on the format and the number of lessons per week; just get in touch.

How to study between lessons

At A2, short but daily practice brings the fastest progress:

  • Learn words in sentences: instead of “der Termin”, write “Ich habe morgen einen Termin.” (I have an appointment tomorrow.) – the article and the usage stick together.
  • Describe your day in German: three sentences in the Perfekt each evening – what you did, where you went, who you talked to.
  • Listen a little every day: a few minutes of slow German news or podcasts, noting words you understood.
  • Do exercises: the A2 exercises on the website; redo the ones you got wrong the next day.

Questions about the A2 course

Can I start A2 without finishing A1?

A2 builds on A1, so we check your level first. If a few A1 topics are missing, we cover them quickly at the start of the A2 lessons.

Can I work in Germany with A2?

That depends on the job and the employer. A2 is a good base for everyday communication. The level and certificates required can vary by application, institution and over time – always check the current requirement with the official source.

What is hardest at A2?

Most learners struggle to tell the dative from the accusative and with adjective endings. We practise these with lots of examples and short, frequent reviews.
