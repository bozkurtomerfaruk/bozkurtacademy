German Course B1

Why B1 matters

B1 is the first step of the CEFR’s “independent user” level. At B1 you understand the main points of conversations about work, school and free time, handle most situations while travelling, describe experiences and plans and give short reasons for your opinions.

With B1 you start using German more independently in daily life, at work and in education. Which level and certificate are required for vocational training, job applications or official procedures depends on the institution and the process, so check the current requirement for your situation.

A few B1 sentences:

  • Obwohl ich müde war, bin ich zum Kurs gegangen. (Although I was tired, I went to class.)
  • Das ist die Firma, bei der ich mich beworben habe. (That’s the company I applied to.)
  • Ich finde es wichtig, jeden Tag ein bisschen zu üben. (I think it’s important to practise a little every day.)

What you learn in the B1 course

Longer, linked sentences

  • Connectors
  • Giving reasons with weil, denn, da
  • Relative clauses
  • Word order

Formal and written German

  • The passive
  • The genitive
  • Indirect and polite questions
  • Infinitive with zu
  • lassen + infinitive (having something done)

Telling stories and hypotheticals

  • Telling stories in the Präteritum
  • Plusquamperfekt
  • Konjunktiv II: hypotheticals
  • darauf, worüber …

All four skills together

Speaking: At B1 short answers are no longer enough. We practise giving your opinion in several sentences, planning something together and giving reasons – useful in the exam and in job interviews.

Writing: A formal email, a complaint or a forum post are typical B1 tasks. We correct your texts together and find the mistakes you make most often.

Reading and listening: With short news items, ads and radio pieces we practise finding the main idea and the details. There are free reading practice sets for Goethe B1, telc B1 and DTZ on the website.

If you are preparing for Goethe-Zertifikat B1, telc Deutsch B1 or the DTZ (Deutsch-Test für Zuwanderer), the lessons follow the exam’s task types. More on the exam preparation page.

How the lessons are planned

Before B1 we check for gaps from A2 – the dative, adjective endings and verb position in subordinate clauses come up constantly at B1. Then we plan around your goal: your exam date, or the conversations and writing you need for work or vocational training.

You can take the lessons one-to-one or in a group. The price depends on the format and the number of lessons per week; just get in touch.

How to study between lessons

  • Write one text a week: an email or a short opinion text. We correct it together, and you keep a list of your typical mistakes.
  • Use connectors on purpose: a few sentences a day with “obwohl”, “trotzdem”, “deshalb” – your speaking becomes more fluent.
  • Consume German content: videos or news on a topic you like; first the main idea, then the details.
  • Speak out loud: talk about a topic for one minute, record yourself and note where you got stuck.

Questions about the B1 course

Is B1 enough to work in Germany?

That depends on the profession, the employer and the process. Always check the required level with the employer or the relevant institution; in class we plan around your goal.

Which part of the B1 exam is hardest?

Usually writing and speaking, because you are expected to give reasons for your opinion. We practise these tasks with useful phrases and lots of practice.

What is the difference between the DTZ and Goethe B1?

The DTZ is designed for immigrants in Germany and is the final exam of the integration course; Goethe-Zertifikat B1 is a general language exam. Which one you need depends on what you will use the certificate for – check current information with the exam provider and the institution you are applying to.
